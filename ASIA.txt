To truly master these Layer 2 and Layer 3 skills, you need a topology that forces protocols to actually do their jobs. In a simple star topology, Spanning Tree (STP) has nothing to block, and EtherChannel isn't necessary.

By adding physical loops and redundant links, we are turning your Asia Engineering site into a **"Hard Mode" enterprise switching lab**. You will practice Trunking, EtherChannel, and STP over and over again.

Here is your upgraded blueprint with the new wiring instructions, 5 VLANs, and advanced edge security.

---

### Phase 1: The "Hard Mode" Wiring Guide

To create this lab, you need to delete a few of the existing single wires in your topology and replace them with redundant links and cross-connections.

**1. The Core & Router Link:**

* Keep the single wire between **R31 (WAN)** and **R27 (Core)**. This will be your 802.1Q Trunk for Router-on-a-Stick.

**2. Build 4 EtherChannels (Dual-Links):**
Delete the single wires connecting R27 to R32, R34, R41, and R35. Replace them with **two wires each**:

* Connect 2 wires between **R27** and **R32** (Port-Channel 1)
* Connect 2 wires between **R27** and **R34** (Port-Channel 2)
* Connect 2 wires between **R27** and **R41** (Port-Channel 3)
* Connect 2 wires between **R27** and **R35** (Port-Channel 4)

**3. Build Spanning Tree Loops (Cross-Connects):**
Leave the remaining switches (R33, R36, R40, R39) connected via single wires, but daisy-chain them to create physical triangles (loops) that will force STP to calculate paths and block ports:

* Connect 1 wire from **R32** to **R33**, and 1 wire from **R33** to **R34**.
* Connect 1 wire from **R41** to **R40**, and 1 wire from **R40** to **R36**.
* Connect 1 wire from **R35** to **R39**.

### Phase 3: How to Execute Your Lab Repetitions

With this new wiring, here is how you will practice your modules:

* **Module 6 (VLANs):** You must create all 5 VLANs (`99, 100, 101, 102, 103`) on **every single switch**. (Great practice for repetitive CLI work, or configuring VTP).
* **Module 3 (Trunking):** Every single wire connecting a switch to another switch (or to the router) must be a trunk. You will practice `switchport mode trunk` and `switchport trunk allowed vlan ...` at least 15 times in this setup.
* **Module 4 (EtherChannel):** You will configure LACP `channel-group X mode active` on R27 four separate times, and once each on R32, R34, R41, and R35.
* **Modules 9 & 10 (STP & MST):** Because you built triangles (e.g., R32 to R33 to R34 back to R27), Spanning Tree will naturally detect a broadcast loop and shut down one of those links. You will configure `R27` as the Primary Root Bridge. You will practice using `show spanning-tree` to hunt down exactly which ports got blocked.
* **Module 7 & 11 (BPDU Guard & Security):** On the 5 specific switch ports pointing to your VPCs, you will configure `switchport mode access`, `switchport access vlan X`, and critically: `spanning-tree portfast` and `spanning-tree bpduguard enable`. (If you ever accidentally plug a switch into one of those VPC ports, BPDU Guard will instantly slam the port shut to protect the network).

This design guarantees you will get massive amounts of hands-on repetition for every single Layer 2 technology on your list.
