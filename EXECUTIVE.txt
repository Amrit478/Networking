# VIP Executive Site — Master Blueprint (Hard Mode Lab)

**Supernets:** `10.4.0.0/16` | `2001:DB8:4::/48`

---

### Phase 1: Physical Wiring & Topology Design

To ensure proper segmentation and allow you to practice trunking and security features:

1. **Firewall to Switch Link:**
* Connect **Fortinet Firewall (`port1`)** to **Switch (`R18`)** on interface `e3/0`. This acts as your secure external/WAN boundary trunk.


2. **Switch to Router Link:**
* Connect **Switch (`R18`)** on interface `e0/1` to **Router (`R42`)** on interface `e0/0`. Configure this single physical wire as an 802.1Q trunk to support **Router-on-a-Stick (Module 8)**.


3. **The 4 VPC Endpoints:**
* Connect **VPC49**, **VPC50**, **VPC58**, and **VPC57** directly into `R42` ports (`e0/2`, `e0/1`, `e0/3`, and `e0/10`) to simulate multi-VLAN user edge ports.



---

### Phase 2: Layer 2 VLAN & Switch Management Setup

#### 1. The 4 Global VLANs (Modules 3, 6, & 9)

Configure these VLANs on switch `R18`:

* **VLAN 99:** Management (`Mgmt`)
* **VLAN 40:** Executive VIP Data (`Exec`)
* **VLAN 41:** VIP Secure Sandbox (`Sandbox`)
* **VLAN 42:** VIP Guest (`Guest`)

#### 2. Switch Management IP Addressing (Module 2)

* **Switch `R18` Management IP:** `10.4.99.11/24` on VLAN 99.
* **Default Gateway:** Set `ip default-gateway 10.4.99.1` on `R18`.

---

### Phase 3: Layer 3 Subinterfaces, DHCP, and Routing (Modules 8, 14, 15–17)

All default gateways, DHCP pools, and dynamic routing terminate on **Router `R42**` using subinterfaces.

#### 1. Router `R42` Subinterfaces & DHCP Server Pools

| Subinterface | VLAN & Purpose | IP Address (Gateway) | DHCP Scope Range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `e0/0.99` | **VLAN 99 (Mgmt)** | `10.4.99.1/24` | *Static Only (No DHCP)* |
| `e0/0.40` | **VLAN 40 (Exec)** | `10.4.40.1/24` | `10.4.40.50` – `10.4.40.200` |
| `e0/0.41` | **VLAN 41 (Sandbox)** | `10.4.41.1/24` | `10.4.41.50` – `10.4.41.200` |
| `e0/0.42` | **VLAN 42 (Guest)** | `10.4.42.1/24` | `10.4.42.50` – `10.4.42.200` |

#### 2. VPCS Access Ports & Dynamic Allocation (Module 7)

The executive endpoints pull their configurations automatically via DHCP through Router `R42`:

* **VPC49** (Connected to R42 `e0/2`): Assigned to VLAN 40 $\rightarrow$ Receives `10.4.40.x`
* **VPC50** (Connected to R42 `e0/1`): Assigned to VLAN 40 $\rightarrow$ Receives `10.4.40.x`
* **VPC58** (Connected to R42 `e0/3`): Assigned to VLAN 41 $\rightarrow$ Receives `10.4.41.x`
* **VPC57** (Connected to R42 `e0/10`): Assigned to VLAN 42 $\rightarrow$ Receives `10.4.42.x`

---

### Phase 4: Enterprise Layer 3 Integration (OSPF & Summarization)

To tie the VIP site into your Data Center and other branch networks via the central ISP/WAN cloud:

* **OSPF Area Configuration (Modules 15–17):**
* Enable OSPF on Router `R42` pointing toward the firewall and core WAN cloud in **Area 0**.
* Use the network statement `network 10.4.0.0 0.0.255.255 area 0`.
* Configure `passive-interface` on all subinterfaces (`e0/0.99`, `e0/0.40`, `e0/0.41`, `e0/0.42`) to prevent routing protocol traffic from bleeding into user-facing subnets.


* **Route Summarization (Module 19):**
* Configure OSPF summary advertisements on Router `R42` to advertise a single clean supernet (`10.4.0.0/16`) to the rest of the enterprise topology.

**3. A logical design flaw in Phase 1, point 3**

This part doesn't quite work as "Hard Mode" practice: **connecting the 4 VPCs directly into the router's physical ports** means each VPC sits on its own dedicated interface — there's no VLAN tagging or trunking happening on those links at all. Router-on-a-Stick and 802.1Q subinterfaces only matter on the **single trunk link** between the switch and the router (which here is just the `R18`–`R42` link). Plugging PCs straight into router ports is actually the *opposite* of what tests trunking — it's old-school "router with multiple physical interfaces," not VLAN segmentation.

If the goal is genuine "Hard Mode" VLAN/trunk practice, the 4 VPCs should instead plug into **access ports on switch `R18`**, each assigned to a different VLAN (40, 41, 41, 42), with the single `R18`–`R42` link carrying all VLANs as an 802.1Q trunk. That way DHCP requests from each VPC actually have to get tagged, cross the trunk, hit the correct subinterface on `R42`, and get answered — which is the real point of Router-on-a-Stick.
